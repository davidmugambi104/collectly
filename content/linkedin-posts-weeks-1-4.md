## Week 2 — Show the system

### Post 4 — Tue

**The 15-minute weekly AR review every agency should run.**

Most agencies review revenue. Few review receivables.

Here's the 15-minute weekly habit that changes cash flow:

1. Open QBO/Xero. Filter invoices by "overdue."
2. Sort by amount, then by age.
3. For each invoice over 14 days overdue, assign one action:
   - send reminder
   - schedule call
   - escalate to collections
   - write off
4. Log the action and the date.

That's it.

The magic isn't the review. It's that you stop pretending you'll remember.

I used to do this manually in a spreadsheet. Now I use Collectly to automate reminders and track which clients are slowest to pay.

If you ran this review this week, how much would be in the overdue column? If you don't know, that's the problem.

**Why your bookkeeper shouldn't be the one chasing clients.**

Bookkeepers are great at keeping records straight.

They are usually not great at asking your biggest client why they haven't paid.

That job belongs to whoever owns the client relationship — usually the founder or account lead. The bookkeeper can flag what's overdue. The founder decides how hard to push.

The mistake I see: founders abdicate AR entirely to finance, then get surprised when cash is tight.

Better setup:
- Bookkeeper: sends the weekly overdue report
- Founder/ops: decides on escalation and handles sensitive clients
- System: sends the predictable reminder emails so nothing falls through

Collectly sits in the system layer — polite, timed, logged. The founder keeps control of the relationship layer.

### Post 8 — Thu

**The difference between an invoice reminder and a payment request.**

A reminder says: "Just checking in."
A request says: "This needs to be paid."

Most agencies send reminders. Then they wonder why clients keep delaying.

Here's the shift:

**Reminder (weak):** "Hi, just following up on invoice #1234. Let me know if you need anything."

**Request (clear):** "Hi, invoice #1234 is 14 days overdue. Can you confirm the payment date? If there's a hold-up, let me know today so we can resolve it."

Same politeness. More clarity. A specific ask.

The goal isn't to be aggressive. It's to remove ambiguity. When clients can ignore you, they do. When they have to respond, most pay.

Collectly automates this escalation so you don't have to write the awkward email yourself.
