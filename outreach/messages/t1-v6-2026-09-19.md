Replaces `t1-cold-v3-industry-variants.md`, which has a measured reply rate of
**0.34%** (1 reply from 292 delivered sends).

## What the data rules out

A four-way subject test already ran inside v3 — variants A, B, C and D at
roughly 73 sends each, 293 in total. **One reply across all four.** So the
subject line is not the lever, and rewriting only the subject would be
answering a question that has already been answered.

The one reply came from P050, on variant B (`Quick question`), after the

## What is actually wrong with v3

**It asks a stranger for a meeting.** "Worth 10 minutes this week? I can send a
few open times." That is the highest-friction thing a first email can request.
It has to clear a bar the email has not earned yet.

**It pitches in the second paragraph.** "I'm building Collectly for small
studios and agencies on QuickBooks who…" — the reader has been given no reason
to care before being told what we sell.

**It advertises risk.** "Early, live, starting with a small batch before broad
launch" is meant to sound exclusive. To a business deciding who chases its
money, it reads as *this might not exist in six months*.

**It opens on a hypothetical.** "Any QBO invoice that's 14+ days overdue —" is
a guess about the reader dressed as a question. If the guess is wrong, the
email is over. If the guess is right, it still sounds like every other tool
email they get.

**It is aimed at the wrong integration.** v3 says QBO throughout. The product
leads with Xero — the homepage says "Connects to Xero", the site declares
QuickBooks in beta, and `/integrations` lists QuickBooks as `sandbox` against
Xero's `live`. We have been cold-emailing about the weaker half of the product.

## v6

Two variants. Same structure, different angle, so the test measures the
*approach* rather than the wording.

### Variant A — the question

**Subject:** `who chases your late invoices?`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

No pitch — an actual question. When a client is three weeks late paying
{{company}}, who does the chasing?

I'm building a tool that sends those reminders automatically, and the part
I keep getting wrong is tone: firm enough to work, not so firm it costs you
the client.

If you already have a way that works, I'd like to hear what it is. If you
don't, that's the more interesting answer.

David
```

### Variant B — the specific

**Subject:** `the Friday payment run`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

"It's approved, it goes out in Friday's payment run." Then Friday passes.

I'm building something that catches that — reads the reply, holds the date,
and restarts the chase on its own if the money doesn't arrive. It connects
to Xero.

Does that happen at {{company}}, or have you got it handled?

David
```

## Why these are shaped this way

**No meeting ask.** Both end on a question answerable in one line, from a
phone, without opening a calendar. The goal of a first email is a reply, not a
booking — and at 0.34% we do not have the standing to ask for thirty minutes.

**No pitch before a reason.** Variant A withholds the product until the third
line. Variant B leads with a sentence the reader has said or heard themselves.

**No hedging.** "Early", "pre-launch", "small batch" are gone. Nothing claims
customers, metrics or case studies, because there are none — but nothing
apologises for that either.

**Xero, not QBO.** Variant B names Xero explicitly, matching what the product
actually does best.

**"Or have you got it handled?"** gives an easy, face-saving no. Cold emails
that only accept yes get neither.

## Send rules

- Subject in lowercase and unpunctuated on purpose: it looks like a person
  typed it, not a campaign tool.
- One send per prospect. v3 emailed one person **eight** times and thirteen
  others twice — 20 duplicate sends. `daily_send.py` has a 14-day cooldown;
  that is clearly not sufficient on its own.
- Personal addresses only. Role addresses bounce at 39.1% against 1.8% and are
  now blocked in the send path.
- Whatever the deliverability gate says is the cap. It is currently pullback,
  30/day.
